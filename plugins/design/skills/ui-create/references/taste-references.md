# Taste references

How widely used products look, observed on their public web pages on 25.11.2026: the first screen of the home page (or the public landing page) at 1280 × 800 and 390 × 844, light theme. These are the products many people already know, so they set the conventions people bring to a new design ("familiar first", `taste.md`). Use them to name a direction and to see which conventions hold across a market; take principles, never the look, logo, illustrations or screens. Screenshots are not kept in this repository.

What this is not: a view of the logged-in apps, of later screens or of the mobile apps; a judgement of their quality. Ratings are one reviewer's reading of one screen on one day; pages change.

## Six families

**1. Quiet utility** — Google, Wikipedia, GOV.UK, eGov.kz, Telegram. White canvas, colour limited to one link or action colour or one flat band; the search field or a list of tasks comes first; no decorative imagery; plain task copy; links obviously links. *Principle: the page is a doorway — one input, short labels, nothing competing with it.*

**2. Loud promo marketplace** — Uzum Market, Wildberries, Ozon, Mercado Libre, AliExpress. One saturated brand colour floods the header or banner; dense rows of product cards with price, old price, discount badge and rating; sticker-like sale art. The frame stays very conventional: catalogue button, wide search, labelled cart and sign-in, a labelled bottom tab bar on phones. *Principle: the frame stays predictable so the content can be loud — the loudness comes from merchants' content, not from the controls.*

**3. Institutional task-first** — HSBC UK, Chase, Kaspi.kz, Payme, Booking.com. Brand colour kept for actions and one hero band; a real task on the first screen (sign-in, phone login, booking search, service shortcuts); icons paired with text labels; filled rectangular buttons; fine print near offers. *Principle: trust through structure.* Seen exceptions: a login field with no visible submit, a desktop page with no action above the fold.

**4. Dark immersive** — Netflix, Spotify, Linear, Uber, Click. Near-black canvas; colour comes from the content (posters, album art, product screens, photos) or one accent; the main action is a single high-contrast pill; fields are filled surfaces, often without borders. *Principle: dark works when the content is visual and the action is one bright pill.* Seen risk: dark plus several gradient pills and unlabelled icons.

**5. Big-type product landing** — Notion, Stripe, WhatsApp, Yandex Go. One very large headline, one or two actions, a lot of space; identity carried by one device (a gradient, a photo, black type). *Principle: one message, one action.* Seen weaknesses: no filled action at all; a main button that looks disabled.

**6. Warm and playful** — Duolingo, Airbnb. Illustration with character, large radii, soft shadows or raised buttons, brand colour used sparingly but memorably. *Principle: playfulness lives in illustration and shape, while labels and actions stay plain.*

## Conventions that hold across these products

- A filled button for the main action: 21 of 26 products. The exceptions were text links, no action above the fold, an outlined button only, and an icon-only search square.
- Bottom tab bars on phone pages carry text labels in almost every case seen (one exception).
- Header controls (search, account, menu) are often icon-only even on conventional sites — the one place the market tolerates unlabelled icons, and the place least experienced users struggle (`ui-rules` EXP-8).
- Fields: some products draw a visible border, others rely on a filled background with weak contrast; both exist, the bordered field is the safer choice for less experienced audiences.
- Phone pages often push people to the native app; cookie, promo and location dialogs on first load were common.

## How to use this

1. Find where the profile's taste sits on the five axes and which family is closest.
2. Check the conventions of the products the audience actually uses (the profile's "audience experience"); where they differ from this list, the audience's products win.
3. Keep controls, navigation and forms as conventional as the least experienced person in the audience needs; let colour, imagery, type and copy carry the taste.
