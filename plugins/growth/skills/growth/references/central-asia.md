# Central Asia and the CIS: what is different

General tools assume English, big sites and Western platforms. Here most of that fails.
The notes below are starting points: platform shares shift every year and differ by city and age,
so confirm the picture for the user's market in step 1 and mark it **seen** only when you have a source.

## Languages and scripts

| Market | Write and search in | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Uzbekistan | Uzbek Latin (`uz-Latn`), Uzbek Cyrillic (`uz-Cyrl`), Russian | Latin is the official script; Cyrillic is still common in news, among older readers and in search queries. Tashkent uses much more Russian than the regions. Karakalpak in Karakalpakstan |
| Kazakhstan | Kazakh (Cyrillic), Russian | both everywhere; a Latin transition is planned but everyday writing is Cyrillic |
| Kyrgyzstan | Kyrgyz (Cyrillic), Russian | |
| Tajikistan | Tajik (Cyrillic), Russian | |
| Wider CIS | Russian plus the national language | |

Rules:

1. **Search every query in each language and script.** `ingliz tili kurslari Samarqand`,
   `инглиз тили курслари Самарқанд`, `курсы английского Самарканд` return different competitors.
2. **Uzbek Latin apostrophes.** Correct letters are oʻ and gʻ with ʻ (U+02BB). People type `o'`, `` o` ``,
   `o’` or skip it (`ozbek`). When searching, try the variants. When writing, use ʻ.
3. **Do not translate, write again.** A Russian headline translated word for word into Uzbek reads as
   foreign. Write each language from the facts. Keep numbers, dates and prices identical.
4. **Local formats.** Prices: `450 000 soʻm` / `450 000 сум`; `₸` in Kazakhstan. Dates: `6-dekabr`, `6 декабря`.
   Phone numbers: `+998 …`, `+7 7…`, `+996 …`, `+992 …`.
5. **Have a native speaker read the final text** — say this to the user once, when handing over content in a language they did not write themselves.
6. Record which language each competitor uses in each channel: a competitor posting only in Russian
   leaves the Uzbek-speaking audience open, and the reverse.

## Where businesses live

| Need | Look at |
|---|---|
| Channel and news of a business | Telegram channel (often instead of a site), Instagram profile |
| Leads | Telegram bot or direct message, Instagram Direct, WhatsApp (strong in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan), phone |
| Maps and reviews | Yandex Maps, 2GIS, Google Maps — check all three per city |
| Classifieds | OLX (`olx.uz`, `olx.kz`), Lalafo (Kyrgyzstan), Somon (Tajikistan); in Kazakhstan vertical boards for cars and housing |
| Marketplaces and super-apps | Uzum (Uzbekistan), Kaspi (Kazakhstan) — shop pages carry ratings and review counts |
| Vacancies | hh (`hh.uz`, `hh.kz`), OLX jobs, Telegram job channels |
| Payments shown on the site | Click, Payme, Uzum (Uzbekistan), Kaspi (Kazakhstan) — a payment button means online sales exist |
| Local news and city media | Telegram channels of the city, local news sites — places where competitors buy posts |
| Education niche | channels of schools and universities, parent chats, exam-prep channels |

Step 1 of a market scan is always "where do these buyers look": ask the user which three places
their last ten customers came from, then check those places first.

## Small sites: when paid tools know nothing

Traffic estimators and keyword databases rarely cover a site with a few hundred visits a day in
Uzbek or Kazakh. Say that plainly, and measure what is public instead:

| Instead of | Use | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic estimate | Telegram views per post, subscribers; map card review counts; Instagram followers if visible | seen |
| Keyword database | Google autocomplete and "People also ask" for the niche in each language; questions in city chats | seen |
| Backlink index | `"<brand>" -site:<domain>`, catalogue mentions of the channel | seen |
| Ad spend estimate | Meta Ad Library active ads and start dates; ad posts in Telegram channels | seen (ads), assumed (spend) |
| Tech lookup service | page source: site builder, trackers, chat and payment widgets | seen |
| Company size | vacancies, number of branches on the map, team page, legal entity in the public register where one exists | seen / assumed |
| History | web archive snapshots, the first post of the channel, the first review on the map | seen |

A site on a builder with no trackers, a Telegram link and a phone number is the normal picture, not
a weak competitor: their funnel is the channel and the phone. Follow it there.

## Offline is a channel

Flyers at partner schools, banners near bazaars and bus stops, open days, exhibitions — they work
and they can be measured: a QR code with its own UTM link per place (`tools/utm`), a separate phone
extension or a promo word. Ask the user what offline they already do before proposing digital only.
Mode (d) prepares an event.

## Regulation — flag, do not advise

Advertising, personal data and education services are regulated, and rules differ by country
(language requirements for ads, consent for publishing students' results, licences). When a move
touches these — a student's name and score, a comparison with a competitor, a medical or financial
promise — flag it: "check local rules or ask a lawyer". Do not state what the law says.
